# Evidence & Source Links

## Why evidence matters

Evidence keeps the radar grounded. A signal should be traceable to a real public source so the dashboard reflects actual developments rather than inferred or fabricated claims.

## Why public source traceability matters

Public source links let a reviewer check what was said, when it was published, and how much confidence should be placed in the signal.

That traceability is important because it makes the dashboard auditable and easier to trust in a portfolio context.

## Primary and secondary sources

Primary or official sources are the strongest inputs. These include company announcements, government publications, regulatory notices, and standards bodies.

Secondary or media analysis can still be useful, but it adds interpretation rather than direct origin. Those signals should be reviewed more carefully and should not be treated as equivalent to primary evidence.

## Why human review still exists

Even an official source does not automatically make a signal operationally useful.

Human review is still needed to decide:

- whether the source is relevant to Profile A
- whether the signal is new or just repeated noise
- whether the recommendation is justified
- whether the signal should stay active, be archived, or become a project candidate

## Current limits

- No backend
- No scraping
- No automatic verification engine
- No AI-generated fake citations
- No charts

The source verification layer is intentionally lightweight and manual.
